Methodology

How the analysis works

Each pathway, cost and readiness rating on this platform comes from GARUDA, a generation & transmission capacity-expansion model that co-optimizes industrial facility investments alongside the PLN grid. This page summarizes that method, drawn from the November 2025 report.

Research objective

The analysis investigates how Indonesian industries with captive coal generators can decarbonize their electricity supply in line with national clean-energy objectives, focusing on near- to mid-term solutions through 2035 for power systems that operate independently of the PLN grid. It compares two main families of decarbonization options — on-site renewables (solar, wind, geothermal, battery storage) and grid connection — to identify the optimal, cost-effective mix for each facility.

Co-optimization approach

Each industrial facility is modeled as an islanded system that can also import power through a grid connection. The main PLN grid is optimized at the same time, so industrial and grid investments are solved together under a single objective that minimizes total system cost. Two parallel tracks — grid and industry — feed a shared least-cost expansion model:

Decarbonization pathways

Results combine two factors: the decarbonization option available to a facility, and the clean-energy target applied. The pathways shown on facility pages map to the report's modeled scenarios:

  • Business as usual — facility stays off-grid and continues operating its coal generators.
  • On-site solar + storage (Captive) — facility builds new captive renewables (solar, wind, geothermal, battery) but does not connect to the grid.
  • Grid connection (Grid) — facility connects to the PLN grid and draws on the existing/expanded grid fleet.
  • Hybrid (GridCaptive) — facility connects to the grid and builds new captive renewables, while keeping existing captive coal. This is the report's lowest-cost decarbonization scenario.

Clean-energy targets layered on top: JETP targets cap grid CO₂ and set a minimum grid renewable share; PerPres 112/2022 (35%R) applies a 35% emissions-reduction requirement to the captive facilities themselves.

The optimization model

GARUDA (Grid And Renewable Utilization & Deployment Analysis) is a generation- and transmission-capacity-expansion mixed-integer linear program (MILP) built in Julia/JuMP, solved with Gurobi on the San Diego Supercomputer Center's Triton cluster.

  • Decision variables: new-build and retired capacity for generators, storage and transmission lines; plus operations — unit commitment of thermal plants, dispatch, storage charging, non-served energy, line flows, and grid imports.
  • Constraints: zonal demand balance, transmission-expansion requirements, dispatch min/max limits, time-coupling across periods, storage and thermal unit-commitment rules (min up/down time), and clean-energy constraints from PerPres 112/22 and JETP.
  • Objective: minimize total annual system cost — annualized capital and fixed costs of new generation, storage and transmission, plus variable operating costs, thermal start-up costs, grid-connection costs, and penalties for non-served demand.
  • Zonal aggregation: each island system modeled separately (Sumatera 9 zones, Jawa-Bali 7, Kalimantan 5, Sulawesi 6; North Maluku, Maluku, Papua and Nusa Tenggara as single zones).
  • Time sampling: eight representative weeks of hourly operation selected by k-means clustering, including a peak week for resource adequacy.

Data sources

  • Power sector: official documents from PLN, the Ministry of Energy & Mineral Resources (MEMR) and the JETP Secretariat. Current/planned generators, transmission and load projections from PLN RUPTL 2021–2030; fuel costs and new-plant projects from the JETP CIPP; demand profile based on the Malaysia Grid System Operator 2023 load profile; supplemented with OpenStreetMap.
  • Industrial facilities: generator data from the Sunrise Project International Indonesian captive power database; synthetic load profiles built per commodity from process literature, with production scaled on historical trends (3–5% growth).
  • Coal generators are categorized as grid, captive, or mine-mouth captive, each with its own coal-price estimate.
  • Sites on this platform: the map and facility profiles (names, locations, sectors, owners and captive coal units by status) come from the Earthwise Institute Indonesian Power Summary Database (Captive Coal Database, v2.0, content as of 31 Dec 2025). Units that are cancelled, shelved, retired or closed are excluded. Each site is linked to GARUDA's industrial-park nodes; sites added since the last model run are marked as not yet modeled. Citation: Earthwise Institute. Indonesian Power Summary Database. Earthwise Institute, 2026.

Renewable resource assessment

Grid renewable potential uses MEMR's assessment based on the National Energy Plan (RUEN). For captive renewables, candidate sites are assessed within a 10 km buffer of each industrial facility:

  • Solar & wind: capacity factors from ERA5 reanalysis data (geodata package).
  • Geothermal: Global Heat Flow Database (IHFC), ordinary-kriging interpolation on a 500 m grid, retaining areas with heat flow ≥ 80 mW/m².
  • Land-use exclusions: forest and residential areas from the Ministry of Environment & Forestry (KLHK); slope constraints from USGS EarthExplorer.

Key assumptions & sensitivities

  • Horizon: investment and operations optimized to 2035.
  • Coal price: a low-price sensitivity at $70/ton reflects the Domestic Market Obligation (DMO) cap.
  • Grid import price: a high-import-price sensitivity adds +21% to the grid import cost.
  • Industrial production growth: 3–5% annually.

Results are highly sensitive to grid electricity tariffs and coal prices — both shift the share of renewables in the optimal mix. The sliders on each facility page let you explore this directly.

Defining transition readiness

The High / Medium / Low readiness rating on the map summarizes how favorable a facility's conditions are for a near-term shift away from captive coal. [Working definition — criteria to be finalized] Candidate factors, drawn from GARUDA's inputs:

  • Quality and sufficiency of local renewable resources within the 10 km buffer (solar, wind, geothermal).
  • Feasibility and cost of grid connection (distance to the PLN network and required upgrades).
  • Scale of the facility's load and remaining life of existing captive coal units.
  • Cost gap between the lowest-cost clean pathway and business-as-usual at current coal/tariff assumptions.

Full detail, figures and the technical appendix are in the November 2025 report; model code is published on GitHub.
